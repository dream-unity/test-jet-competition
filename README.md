# Apex Relational Racing

**Dream Unity — one continuous causal-relational fighter-jet competition**

[Play the deployed GitHub Pages build](https://dream-unity.github.io/test-jet-competition/)

Apex Relational Racing is a self-contained browser-native 3D fighter race in which relational reasoning is expressed through the aircraft's continuous trajectory. It contains no picture analogies, answer rings, symbolic premises, hidden answer sides or arbitrary timed control remaps.

The player flies one complete six-jet race through ten progressively harder relational levels. Correctly entering the causal airflow relation produces acceleration, afterburner recovery and persistent Flow Drive. Misreading it creates drag and competitive separation.

## One race, ten levels

There is one game mode and one finish line. The ten levels form a single learning-and-transfer sequence:

1. **Vortex Reading** — predictive convergence with maximum causal evidence and an embodied demonstration.
2. **Formation Axis** — leader-relative reflection with a second embodied demonstration.
3. **Moving Frame** — transfer a relation through a visibly rotating reference frame.
4. **Temporal Window** — act within an interval generated by the ordering of visible race events.
5. **Energy Intercept** — combine relative speed, altitude, energy and course curvature.
6. **Live Race Role** — update the reference object as race order changes.
7. **Predictive Vortex** — solve vortex convergence with reduced assistance.
8. **Advanced Reference Frame** — transform relations under lower-evidence conditions.
9. **Vortex × Frame** — held-out composition of prediction and moving-frame transformation.
10. **Formation × Time** — held-out spatial-temporal composition under maximum competition pressure.

Visual assistance is strongest in Levels 1–2, then progressively withdrawn. Levels 9–10 test composition rather than opening a separate transfer mode.

## Core loop

**Observe the race → infer the relation → predict its transformation → commit a flight path → enter the physical field → update the model → compete.**

The rendered evidence and the scoring rule use the same observable race state. No solution depends on a private answer token or unrendered random polarity.

## Direct, fluid controls

Every input device uses the same default convention:

- **W / Arrow Up / stick-up / mouse-up:** climb;
- **S / Arrow Down / stick-down / mouse-down:** dive;
- **A / D or Left / Right:** coordinated fighter turn with bank and yaw assistance;
- **Q / E:** independent manual roll for barrel rolls and inverted flight;
- **Z / X:** independent rudder yaw;
- **R / F:** throttle up/down;
- **Shift:** afterburner;
- **Space:** airbrake;
- **C:** change chase camera;
- **P / Escape:** pause.

Mouse steering is relative to the point where the drag begins rather than absolute screen position. Precision deadzones and a nonlinear response curve preserve fine corrections near centre while retaining full authority at the edge. Direct pitch is the default; a persistent menu toggle enables inverted pitch.

Touch uses a left climb/turn stick, a right yaw/throttle stick and independent afterburner/airbrake buttons. Gamepad, keyboard, mouse and touch all feed the same control-composition layer.

## Fighter flight

The v5 runtime uses quaternion-oriented arcade fighter dynamics:

- true three-dimensional position, velocity, orientation and angular velocity;
- independent pitch, roll and yaw;
- coordinated turns, bank-and-pull manoeuvres, loops, barrel rolls and inverted flight;
- thrust aligned with the nose;
- throttle, afterburner, airbrake, lift, drag and gravity;
- angle of attack, sideslip, stalls, G-load and manoeuvre energy loss;
- climb/dive energy exchange and speed-sensitive authority;
- substepped physics for high-speed stability;
- body-relative chase camera.

The jet launches at **500 m/s (1,800 km/h)**. The race configuration supports up to **1,120 m/s (4,032 km/h; approximately Mach 3.27)** under sufficient thrust and Flow Drive.

The old countdown bug that held the airbrake before launch is removed. Countdown preserves speed, orientation and throttle.

## Circuit

The procedural Catmull-Rom circuit exceeds **48 km**, provides a wide three-dimensional manoeuvring envelope and uses Mach-speed curvature to bank visible course frames. Twenty distributed gates guide the route.

A missed gate never freezes progress, reverses the aircraft or pulls it magnetically backward. It applies a small speed/shield/Flow-Drive penalty and the race continues immediately.

## Fighter airframes

Aircraft are generated as multi-component volumetric meshes rather than triangle arrowheads. The renderer constructs:

- tapered elliptical fuselage;
- one or two engine bodies;
- physical exhaust nozzles and afterburner plumes;
- curved canopy;
- swept wings with thickness;
- tailplanes, canards and single/twin vertical fins;
- intake bodies, wingtip lights, wakes and contrails;
- three differentiated fictional airframes with distance-based detail reduction.

All geometry and audio are generated from repository code. There are no downloaded models, textures, fonts, analytics or runtime libraries.

## Causal relational families

### Predictive vortex convergence

Two racers generate visible inner and outer wingtip-vortex trajectories. The efficient field is the future convergence of the appropriate vortices, not either jet's current position. Error models include current-state chasing, single-wake capture, inverse-vortex selection, reversed prediction and the wrong racer pair.

### Leader-axis formation

The leader and highest-ranked wingman define a visible formation axis. The efficient slot is the wingman's position reflected through the leader's body-relative axis. It moves continuously with bank, formation motion and leader changes.

### Rotating reference frame

A visible rotor supplies a moving local coordinate system. The player transforms a racer-relative relation into the rotor's predicted orientation at arrival. Diagnostic models distinguish current-frame capture, world-frame substitution, inversion and wrong-reference selection.

### Temporal relay

A physical aperture activates during an interval generated by two visible racers crossing two visible boundaries. Early, late, spatial-only and reversed-order trajectories are independently identifiable.

### Relative-energy interception

A visibly boosting racer, relative altitude, lateral motion, player energy and upcoming course curvature jointly determine the energy-efficient intercept.

### Live competitive role

The operative object is a visible role such as the current leader, not a fixed colour. Overtaking can change the correct trajectory during the event.

### Composed transfer

Levels 9–10 combine vortex prediction with a rotating frame, and formation reflection with a temporal window. Their components are encountered independently earlier in the same race.

## Measurement architecture

The game separates three channels:

1. **Relational model selection** — pre-feedback trajectory samples are fitted against the correct causal model and explicit diagnostic alternatives.
2. **Flight execution** — after commitment but before field feedback, movement is scored against the model the player actually selected, normalized by an estimated motor-noise baseline.
3. **Physical consequence** — time inside correct and diagnostic fields changes acceleration, drag, afterburner energy, Flow Drive and race position.

This permits a player to infer incorrectly yet execute that mistaken model precisely, or infer correctly but fly it poorly.

## Practice-effect resistance

- continuous trajectories rather than a finite answer bank;
- procedural course and event geometry;
- live race roles and opponent states;
- cross-session structural-signature avoidance;
- counterbalanced side and altitude;
- transform-equivariant rules;
- generative cognitive-error models rather than decorative distractors;
- progressively withheld evidence;
- held-out relation compositions in Levels 9–10;
- no consistently predictive colour, side, identity or environment.

## AI competitors

AI racers have separate prediction, frame-transformation, temporal, role-updating, composition, inference-latency and motor profiles. They choose among the same causal and diagnostic models and conceal their relational line until player commitment.
